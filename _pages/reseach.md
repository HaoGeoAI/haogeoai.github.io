My research integrates **GeoAI, remote sensing, computer vision, and spatial analysis** to support environmental monitoring and conservation applications. I focus on AI-driven wildlife monitoring using drone thermal imagery and camera trap data, with an emphasis on automated detection, species recognition, ecological interpretation, and spatial analysis of human–wildlife interactions.

<div class="research-tagline">
From detection to ecological interpretation through GeoAI, remote sensing, and multimodal learning.
</div>

---

## Research and Knowledge Base

<div class="project-card">

### GeoAI and Spatial Data Science

I apply geospatial artificial intelligence, machine learning, and spatial data science to analyze environmental systems and conservation landscapes. My work emphasizes the integration of spatial thinking with deep learning methods to generate interpretable ecological information from large-scale visual and geospatial datasets.

</div>

<div class="project-card">

### Remote Sensing for Environmental Monitoring

My research uses remote sensing data from drones, camera traps, and other sensing platforms to support environmental monitoring. I am particularly interested in how drone thermal imagery and ground-based camera trap imagery can provide complementary perspectives for wildlife detection and ecological analysis.

</div>

<div class="project-card">

### Wildlife Monitoring with Drones and Camera Traps

I work with multi-perspective wildlife monitoring systems that combine aerial drone observations and ground-based camera trap data. These systems support species detection, instance counting, activity analysis, and spatial assessment of wildlife distributions across complex conservation landscapes.

</div>

<div class="project-card">

### Vision–Language Models for Ecological Interpretation

I investigate how vision–language models can move ecological AI beyond bounding-box detection toward richer structured interpretation. This includes species identification, instance counting, behavior description, OCR-based metadata extraction, ecological captioning, visual question answering, and higher-level ecological reasoning.

</div>

<div class="project-card">

### Spatial and Spatiotemporal Analysis

I use spatial and spatiotemporal methods to analyze wildlife distributions and human–wildlife interactions. My work includes kernel density estimation, inverse distance weighting, landscape stratification, daily presence records, and temporal proximity analysis to identify ecological patterns across space and time.

</div>

## Current Research Projects

<div class="project-card">

### CameraTrap-Instruct: Vision–Language Modeling for Camera Trap Imagery

CameraTrap-Instruct is a vision–language framework for structured ecological interpretation of camera trap imagery. The framework supports species identification, instance counting, behavior classification, OCR-based metadata extraction, visual question answering, and ecological reasoning.

**Methods:** Vision–language models, LoRA, multimodal projector alignment, camera trap image analysis  
**Applications:** Wildlife monitoring, biodiversity assessment, ecological interpretation  
**Status:** Manuscript in preparation

</div>

<div class="project-card">

### Thermal Vision–Language Model Adaptation for Drone-Based Wildlife Monitoring

This project investigates how RGB-pretrained vision–language models can be adapted to thermal drone imagery for wildlife species recognition and instance counting. The framework uses lightweight multimodal projector alignment and instruction tuning to improve performance in the thermal remote sensing domain.

**Methods:** Thermal remote sensing, VLM adaptation, LoRA, drone imagery, species counting  
**Applications:** Aerial wildlife monitoring, conservation remote sensing, ecological AI  
**Status:** Manuscript in preparation

</div>

<div class="project-card">

### Multi-Perspective Wildlife Monitoring Using Drones and Camera Traps

This project integrates drone thermal imagery and camera trap observations to map wildlife distributions and assess human–wildlife interactions across conservation landscapes.

**Methods:** YOLO detection, drone thermal imagery, camera traps, KDE, IDW, spatial analysis  
**Applications:** Conservation planning, human–wildlife interaction assessment, environmental remote sensing  
**Status:** Manuscript in preparation

</div>
